If you need immediate help

If someone is in immediate danger, call 911. For emotional distress or suicidal crisis, contact 988 anytime.

  • Call or text: 988
  • Chat: 988lifeline.org/chat
  • Veterans: call 988, then press 1

Informational mental health disorders

Recognize signs early and seek support sooner

Anxiety Disorders

Ongoing fear and worry can disrupt work, school, sleep, and relationships.

  • Persistent racing thoughts or dread
  • Restlessness and concentration issues
  • Panic symptoms like shortness of breath

Depression

Depression can affect mood, energy, motivation, and physical health.

  • Low mood for most of the day
  • Loss of interest in usual activities
  • Sleep and appetite changes

Trauma and PTSD

Trauma responses can remain active long after a stressful or dangerous event.

  • Flashbacks, nightmares, or avoidance
  • Feeling constantly on edge
  • Strong emotional or body reactions

When to seek professional care

Reach out when symptoms impact daily function, safety, or quality of life.

  • Symptoms last longer than two weeks
  • Self-help tools are not enough
  • Thoughts of self-harm or hopelessness increase

Online counseling appointment scheduler

Request a counseling session

Non-emergency requests only. For immediate crisis support, use 988 or 911.

What to expect

  • Initial intake and matching to support options.
  • Virtual and in-person pathways when available.
  • Follow-up resources after first appointment.

Before your first session

  • Write 2 to 3 goals for care.
  • Track mood, sleep, and stress patterns.
  • Bring medication and previous treatment history.
